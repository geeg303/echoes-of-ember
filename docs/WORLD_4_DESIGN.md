# World 4 Design — The Crown of Cinders

**Status:** PROPOSED working name and nonlinear structure.

## Identity

World 4 combines vegetation reclaiming machinery, Glassreach architecture, buried structures, open sky, Ember conduits, towers, and ancient flame. Theme: **control versus freedom**. Mechanical posture: **master**. It introduces few new mechanics; challenge comes from fluent combinations of World 1 movement/combat/interactables, Ember Currents, Ember Polarity, secrets, and deliberate power-up choices.

## Map structure

**4-1 The Cinder Gate** establishes the central network and unlocks three branches in any order:

- **4-2A Archive of Flame:** knowledge/memory mastery; polarity and evidence routing.
- **4-2B Warden Foundry:** combat/environment mastery; currents, armor, production machinery.
- **4-2C The Ember Wells:** traversal/renewal mastery; vertical currents, living Ember, Veil evidence.

Completing all three unlocks **4-3 The Last Conduit**, then the **Final Sanctum**. Recommended default: free branch order, persistent completed branch markers, all three required. Existing map graphs support branches; an all-prerequisites unlock predicate and multi-world save registry are likely minor extensions.

## Narrative progression

Cinder Gate establishes the First Keeper's continuing authority. Each branch provides one necessary perspective: why control was created, how it perpetuates itself, and what decentralized survival looks like. Last Conduit forces Nova to reject simply restarting central flow. The Final Sanctum allows the Keeper to make its strongest evidence-based case: past Ember instability caused real suffering, and its system prevented worse outcomes at terrible long-term cost.

## Level-design rules

- Every sequence combines known ideas before escalating speed/density.
- Branch opening challenges communicate expected emphasis but never require a particular branch order.
- Power-ups offer approaches, not locks; expiration cannot strand Nova.
- Checkpoints follow mastery sequences rather than every obstacle.
- Old enemies return in combinations that change decisions; avoid inflated health.
- Each branch has a Veil thread and reconnects without missable campaign-critical truth.

## Final encounter

1. **The First Keeper:** traditional readable boss combat, using Warden vocabulary and environment control.
2. **The Network:** traversal/combat through reconfiguring currents and polarity, with established threats and checkpoint safety.
3. **The Ember:** reach and redirect the source while the Keeper resists; player action enacts decentralization rather than selecting a morality menu.

The ending gradually returns flow to regions. Wardens lose central authority. Change, danger, and responsibility continue.

## Enemy/content envelope

Prefer existing and Worlds 2–3 families in mastered combinations. Add at most one or two roles only if the final network lacks a necessary pressure type. Approximate structure is entry + three branches + final approach + final encounter, with 12–16 secrets distributed across branch identities. Exact level sizes, collectible totals, final enemy roster, and encounter phases remain OPEN QUESTIONS.

## NPC convergence

Use the cast at decisions, refuges, or aftermaths rather than placing all four in every branch. Recommended: each branch foregrounds at most one perspective; a pre-final convergence permits disagreement without making NPC approval the win condition. Vesper's optional material deepens confidence in decentralization; the normal path remains coherent.
